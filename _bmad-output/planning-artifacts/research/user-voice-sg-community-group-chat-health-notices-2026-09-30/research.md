# User-voice research: group-chat delivery and community messaging

**Decision this research serves:** is a ready-to-post dengue message for a group-chat admin a realistic delivery channel, and what can the slides claim?

## Executive summary

**Verdict: plausible, but not proven. Describe it as a proposed channel, and test it with one real conversation.**

1. **The channel is plausible.** WhatsApp is widely used in Singapore (about 80 to 90% monthly, aggregator figures) [1], resident-run Telegram groups exist as neighbourhood information hubs [2], and government bodies use chat groups for engagement [3]. Reuters Institute's 2025 report says WhatsApp use for news in Singapore is stable [4].
2. **Direct evidence is missing.** No source was found showing that RC, constituency or condo chats circulate official notices, that NEA has used chat groups for dengue outreach, or that older residents rely on them. The PA page describes RCs as helping "disseminate information" but does not mention chats [5]. So "residents are informed through group chats" is your team's observed practice, not a sourced fact.
3. **Community messaging on dengue has weak, mostly indirect evidence.** A 2025 meta-review of 15 systematic reviews found only modest effects on mosquito indices, called the evidence "weak", and found most outcomes were mosquito counts, not disease [7]. NEA's own outreach (5,000 volunteers, house visits) reports activity counts, not outcomes [6].
4. **Biggest caveat:** an admin posting an unofficial message carries no official authority, and forwarded-message misinformation is a documented risk [8]. The design's source line, date and "valid until" are the right response.

## Dimension: complaint and workaround patterns

Nothing dengue-specific found. The nearest evidence is on misinformation: a 2020 Singapore study of 151 adults found 52.3% forwarded at least one COVID message and about 13.9% were "chronic" forwarders, on a young, mostly female sample and a COVID context [8]. CheckMate, a volunteer WhatsApp fact-check bot, says older people receive and forward dubious group-chat messages (the service's own framing) [10].

## Dimension: their language

Two positive resident quotes about Telegram groups build "kampung spirit" [2]; they say nothing about notices or dengue. A forwarded-misinformation quote came from an opinion piece with stereotyped language and is low confidence, so I would not use it. **No verbatim resident or volunteer language about dengue notices was found**, so the conversation you plan is the real gap-filler.

## Cross-dimension insights

- The channel is used, but **the specific claim "RC and constituency chats circulate official notices" has no source**. That is exactly what your planned conversation and one slide line should address.
- Because effect evidence is weak and indirect, **the slides should promise a delivery mechanism, not a behaviour change**. This matches your existing "no effect sizes" rule.

## Recommendations

1. **Say "proposed channel", not "how residents get information"** on the slides. Confidence: high (no source found).
2. **Have the one real conversation**, and use its quote instead of asserting the channel. Ask specifically whether chat admins post official notices and what would stop them. Binds to the evidence section of `pitch/round1-outline.md`.
3. **Keep the source line, date, valid-until and "forecast, not a diagnosis"** on every post, because forwarded messages spread misinformation and an unofficial post lacks authority [8]. Medium confidence.
4. **Do not cite the 80% WhatsApp figure as a fact** without a primary source; say "widely used" and cite Reuters if you want one line [4]. Low confidence in the number.
5. **Optionally note** that NEA already uses grassroots volunteers for dengue house visits [6], which supports community-led messaging as a fit, while being clear that this is an activity count and not proof of effect.

## Open questions

- Do RC, constituency or condo chats circulate official notices, in which languages, and do older residents rely on them? Needs one real conversation or a PA / news source.
- Can NEA or myENV alerts be shared or pasted by a group admin (go.gov.sg links exist [11])? Check.
- Current senior messaging-app use (IMDA 2025 report).
