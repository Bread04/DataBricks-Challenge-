## Q1 Channel usage and group chats

1. WhatsApp is the most-used social/messaging app in Singapore, about 80% monthly use (30.4% name it their favourite). Sources: search summaries citing We Are Social / DataReportal and Meltwater (https://wearesocial.com/sg/blog/2025/02/digital-2025-top-digital-and-social-media-trends-in-singapore/, https://www.meltwater.com/en/blog/social-media-statistics-singapore). Publisher: aggregators. Date 2025. Confidence: medium-low. Class: usage-statistic. Search summary only; one summary gave about 90%, which conflicts with 80.1%, so treat as roughly 80 to 90%. No official Singapore statistic retrieved. Telegram-specific usage: not found.
2. Resident-run Telegram groups for new BTO estates act as information hubs (renovation tips, HDB policy, queue numbers, group buys, volunteering). Sizes from a few hundred to thousands; one Tengah group about 10,000 and another 1,300. Usually started by residents before balloting, open to the public, some residents volunteer to manage sub-groups. Telegram allows up to 20,000 members versus WhatsApp's 2,000 (as stated by the article). Source: https://www.hardwarezone.com.sg/lifestyle/apps/new-bto-home-owners-are-finding-kampung-spirit-telegram-groups. Publisher: HardwareZone, reproducing The Straits Times of 21 Jul 2025. Date 22 Jul 2025. Confidence: medium-high. Class: usage/behaviour. Read via page fetch. Covers resident-run groups, not official RC or grassroots chats.
3. RCs, per PA, "help disseminate information and gather feedback"; PA has over 1,800 grassroots organisations and more than 25,000 volunteer leaders (as of 2017); over 650 RCs. Source: https://www.pa.gov.sg/our-network/grassroots-organisations/residents-committees/ (search summary). Publisher: People's Association. Confidence: medium. Class: structural. Search summary only; figure is old (2017). No mention of chat groups on that page.
4. The government itself uses chat groups for engagement: REACH runs a WhatsApp chat group (full, registration closed) and a Telegram group. Source: https://www.reach.gov.sg/latest-happenings/whatsapp-chat-group/. Publisher: REACH. Page last updated 20 Dec 2024. Confidence: high. Class: usage. Not a neighbourhood or RC chat.
5. Seniors: Singapore Digital Society Report 2023 found 86% of seniors use the internet to find information and 92% can communicate online. Source: search summary of IMDA report (https://www.imda.gov.sg/-/media/imda/files/infocomm-media-landscape/research-and-statistics/singapore-digital-society-report/singapore-digital-society-report-2023.pdf). Date 4 Nov 2023, outside the 18-month window. Confidence: low-medium. Search summary only; definition of "seniors" and apps used not verified.

Source: https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/index/peoples-association-grassroots-volunteers-to-partner-nea-and-sg-clean-ambassadors-in-fight-against-dengue. Publisher: NEA. Date 2 to 3 Jul 2022. Source: NEA news pages in the search results (https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/index/people's-association-grassroots-volunteers-to-partner-dengue-prevention-volunteers-in-the-fight-against-dengue). Confidence: medium. Search summary only. Activity count, not outcome.
8. The official Dengue Community Alert System notifies via the myENV app, a web portal (go.gov.sg/dengue-high-risk) and colour-coded banners (purple, red, yellow, green). Source: https://www.nea.gov.sg/dengue-zika/dengue/dengue-community-alert-system. Publisher: NEA. Confidence: high. Class: channel. The page does not describe grassroots or chat roles. ## Q3 Forwarded messages and misinformation

10. Singapore study of 151 adults (mean age 36.4; 17 Mar to 7 May 2020): 94.7% had at least one COVID-related WhatsApp use in a week; 52.3% forwarded at least one COVID message; participants received 2.3 times more than they forwarded; about 13.9% were "chronic" forwarders (about 14 messages per week, mean age 44.1). A search summary added that nearly half of messages were partly or wholly inaccurate (not confirmed in the fetch). Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8709420/. Peer-reviewed via PMC, about 2021. Confidence: high for sample figures. Class: risk. Limits: young, mostly female sample; languages not specified; COVID, not dengue; older residents not sampled.
11. CheckMate is a volunteer-run, non-government WhatsApp fact-check bot (launched 2023) aiming to help older people, who it says receive dubious messages through group chats and forward them. Sources: https://www.asiaone.com/singapore/service-fact-checks-whatsapp-messages-hopes-double-volunteers-ahead-general-election and https://checkmate.sg/about. Confidence: medium. Search summary only. The older-user claim is the service's own framing, not measured. ScamShield exists for scams (not read).
12. (Assistant's inference, not a sourced finding) Resident-created BTO groups are open to the public with volunteer moderators, so an unofficial admin posting a dengue notice would not carry official authority.

Not found: languages used in chats; whether older residents rely on RC chats specifically.

## Q4 Effectiveness

13. A 2025 meta-review of 15 systematic reviews found community mobilisation gave modest changes in mosquito indices: Household Index -0.10 (95% CI -0.20 to 0.00), Container Index -0.03 (-0.05 to -0.01), Breteau Index -0.13 (-0.22 to -0.05). The authors call the evidence "weak", note only 3 of 15 reviews were randomised-trial-only, that education effects are hard to separate from combined interventions, that most outcomes are mosquito indices not disease, and that evidence is insufficient to recommend a method. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12501563/. Date 21 Aug 2025. Confidence: medium-high (page fetch summary; full tables not read). Class: outcome evidence.
14. Other reviews (search summary only) say COMBI programmes improved knowledge, attitudes and practice and reduced breeding sites, but evidence for community-based programmes is weak overall; an India review was inconsistent. Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12059817/, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK74424/. Confidence: low-medium. Not opened.

No Singapore-specific evaluation of chat-based or messaging-based dengue outreach found.

## Leads

- PA pages or Straits Times / CNA / Mothership pieces on grassroots leaders using WhatsApp groups for resident notices and house-visit follow-up; PA "Residents' Network" and "Our Grassroots" apps.
- NEA dengue outreach press releases 2023 to 2026 for any messaging or social-media element, and the NEA SMS / advisory letter mechanism.
- Whether NEA or myENV offers shareable dengue alert content or go.gov.sg links a group admin could paste.
- RSIS "Curbing Falsehoods on WhatsApp and Telegram" and the arXiv CheckMate paper (https://arxiv.org/html/2403.12913v1) for language and older-user detail.
- IMDA Digital Society Report 2025 for current senior messaging-app use.
- Reddit r/singapore or HardwareZone threads on RC or condo chats, for candid voices (beware loud voices).

## Not found

- Any official statistic on Telegram usage in Singapore, or a primary-source survey (with date and sample) for WhatsApp usage.
- Any official PA, government or news statement that RC, MP or town-level chats are used to circulate notices. PA pages describe the RC role generally and do not mention chat groups. The NEA outreach page also had no messaging mention. The condo / MCST search returned only dispute and legal pages.
- Any NEA or partner dengue campaign using WhatsApp or Telegram groups, or an evaluation of such a channel.
- Official Singapore guidance on handling forwarded messages inside community chats (beyond CheckMate / ScamShield tools).
- Languages used in neighbourhood chats, and evidence that older residents rely on them.
- Verbatim resident or volunteer language about dengue notices in chat groups.

## Assistant's overall read

The delivery channel is plausible (WhatsApp widely used; resident Telegram groups exist), but direct evidence for RC or condo chat use and for effectiveness of chat-delivered dengue messaging is missing. Risks: an unofficial copy-paste post lacks authority; forwarded-message misinformation culture; community-engagement effects on dengue are weak and mostly measured on mosquito indices.
